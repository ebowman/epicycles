\title{Ptolemy's epicycles as the geocentric Fourier--Bessel expansion of Newtonian orbits:\\
an explicit transform, error bounds, and a frequency-analysis demonstration}

\author{E. Bowman}
\email{ebowman@boboco.ie}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The idea that Ptolemaic epicycles are ``just Fourier series'' is old but usually stated loosely. We make it precise for Newtonian motion. A bound Kepler orbit is an exact Fourier series in the mean anomaly with Bessel-function coefficients, and each term is a pair of counter-rotating circles; the geocentric position of a body is the sum of two such families, one for the body and one for the Earth, and is almost-periodic rather than periodic. We give the explicit three-dimensional transform from orbital elements to epicycle radii, planes and rates, with rigorous truncation bounds. Truncations reach $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-9}$ AU with about $6$ to $10$ harmonics per orbit for Mars and Ceres but need hundreds at eccentricity $0.89$; a Ptolemaic-order truncation of Mars reproduces all retrograde loops with longitude errors up to $33^\circ$. Numerical frequency analysis (NAFF) recovers the analytic terms from Keplerian data with relative frequency error $\sim2\times10^{-10}$. For an N-body Ceres trajectory over $200$ years the interior-window error falls monotonically with the number of lines to $5\times10^{-5}$ AU, but the full-window error is edge-limited at a few $10^{-2}$ AU and is comparable to a fitted six-element Kepler orbit. The representation holds for bounded quasi-periodic motion on finite windows and fails for unbound orbits and long-term chaos. We also provide an interactive three-dimensional viewer. The conceptual correspondence is not new; our contribution is the explicit quantitative geocentric construction.
\end{abstract}
