# External review of paper/main.pdf (received 2026-10-15)

Verbatim review text as received. Revision tasks: epic "Paper revision per 2026-10-15 review" in bd.

**The paper has a solid analytic core and a useful pedagogical contribution, but I would recommend substantive revision before submission.** The main issues are an overstated high-eccentricity scaling claim, inconsistent statements about perturbed motion, and an uneven numerical comparison.

The reviewer read all 16 pages of paper/main.pdf, checked the central derivations, inspected the figures, and independently reproduced the observed thresholds in Table I. They did not rerun the full simulation package.

The strongest parts are the explicit Fourier–Bessel coefficients, the construction of the geocentric chain, and the uniform truncation bound. The treatment of prior work is careful, and the disclosure that NAFF's full-window performance does not beat fitted Kepler is valuable. These give the paper credibility.

Main feedback, in priority order:

1. **Correct the claim about harmonic count as eccentricity approaches one.**
   *Section IV F, p. 6; repeated pp. 10 and 13.*

   The statement N ∝ (1-e)^{-3/2} describes the geometric decay scale and the sufficient truncation order supplied by your bound. It is **not the asymptotic behavior of the actual minimum order at fixed normalized position tolerance**.

   Taking the limit in your own coefficients gives c_{±k} → J'_k(k)/k = O(k^{-5/3}), so the limiting position tail decays algebraically as O(N^{-2/3}). The derivative asymptotic follows from DLMF's uniform Bessel expansions (https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.20#E7).

   An independent coefficient calculation at tolerance 0.01a gives N=1058 at e=0.999, 1327 at 0.9999, and 1366 at 0.999999, approaching a finite limiting value near 1367.

   **Fix:** distinguish the order certified by Eq. (16) from the actual minimum order, and state which quantities are fixed in the asymptotic argument. This does not invalidate your e=0.89 results or the bound itself.

2. **Separate exact quasi-periodic motion from finite-window approximations.**
   *Section IV G, pp. 6–7; discussion, p. 13.*

   "Near-integrable bounded motion is quasi-periodic" is too broad. KAM results require additional hypotheses and concern surviving invariant tori; they do not establish that an arbitrary bounded trajectory—or this particular Ceres integration—lies on one.

   Conversely, under the analytic invariant-torus assumptions already stated for Eq. (18), the expansion is exact and uniformly convergent for all time. That contradicts the subsequent statement that exact, uniform-in-time representation holds "only" for unperturbed Kepler motion. Laskar's cited treatment (https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0305364) makes this distinction explicitly.

   **Fix:** consistently distinguish exact Kepler expansions, exact expansions on analytic invariant tori, and empirical finite-window approximations of other trajectories. The Ceres experiment demonstrates reconstruction accuracy, not quasi-periodicity.

3. **Make the Ceres baseline comparison more comparable.**
   *Sections V E and VI E, pp. 9, 11–12.*

   NAFF fits the entire perturbed geocentric trajectory, including Earth's perturbations. The six-element baseline adjusts only Ceres while holding Earth to a fixed Kepler orbit. Some baseline error therefore comes from an Earth model that the fit cannot correct.

   There is also an objective mismatch: NAFF uses a strongly center-weighted Hann window, while the Kepler fit minimizes unweighted full-window error. Highlighting the interior comparison favors the region NAFF was optimized to fit.

   **Fix:** either fit both Earth and Ceres jointly, or compare heliocentric Ceres reconstructions and subtract the same integrated Earth trajectory from both. Match the fitting weights, then retain your existing full/interior reporting.

   The reported residuals remain useful. What needs qualification is calling this the "fair" baseline and attributing the improvement specifically to Ceres's representation.

4. **Align the historical framing with the experiment actually performed.**
   *Title, abstract, Table II, and Figure 3.*

   The prominent 33° error belongs to model A: two circular terms without eccentric/equant corrections. It is not an assessment of Ptolemy's actual model. You explain that later, but the title and abstract establish a stronger historical association than the experiment supports.

   **Fix:** call model A the "circular two-term approximation" in the abstract and captions. A more accurate title would be *Geocentric epicycle expansions of Keplerian orbits: explicit coefficients, error bounds, and frequency analysis*.

   The opening description of Ptolemy using "nothing but uniform circular motions" also needs reconciliation with your own explanation of the equant.

5. **Make the quantitative results independently reproducible.**
   *Sections V D–F and VII.*

   "Code availability" lists filenames but supplies no repository URL, archived release, or commit. The viewer also lacks a direct address. These are material omissions when the implementation and visualization are part of the contribution.

   Add a permanent code archive, exact initial conditions and epochs, dependency versions, and commands that reproduce the tables and figures.

   Also report a tighter-tolerance check on the **actual perturbed 200-year integration**. The current 25-year, zero-perturber control does not establish the numerical reference's accuracy over the main experiment. This is missing validation, rather than evidence that the integration is wrong.

Smaller technical corrections:

- **Proposition 4, p. 5:** the spectrum is *contained in* the union of the two harmonic ladders. Equality can fail through vanishing coefficients or cancellation. Circular orbits lose higher harmonics; identical orbits cancel completely. The minimal-period claim likewise needs to refer to frequencies surviving subtraction.
- **Equation (18) and NAFF conversion, pp. 7–8:** a general real vector harmonic traces an ellipse, not necessarily a circle. Add the general ellipse-to-two-circles construction; Proposition 3 currently establishes the Kepler case with orthogonal axes. An SVD of [Re A, -Im A] supplies the axes and circle radii (σ1 ± σ2)/2.
- **Circle counts, pp. 10–12 and conclusion:** k=0 is a static displacement. Use "2N rotating circles plus an offset," rather than 2N+1 circles. The two geocentric offsets can be combined.
- **"True supremum on a grid," pp. 6 and 9:** either say "sampled maximum" or prove where the maximum occurs. There is a promising strengthening here: positivity of the Kepler coefficients allows the single-orbit tail maximum to be attained at M=0.
- **Laplace limit, p. 4:** 0.6627 is a worst-case convergence statement over mean anomaly, not the radius at every fixed M; at M=0, E=0 identically.
- **Frequency resolution, pp. 7–8:** describe 2π/T as a practical resolution/conditioning scale for the method, rather than a universal impossibility threshold.

Editorially: shorten the repeated novelty disclaimers and use that space for a compact table identifying the ten recovered NAFF lines, including amplitude and phase errors. A residual-versus-time plot would also make the endpoint problem much easier to understand than the aggregate error curves alone.
